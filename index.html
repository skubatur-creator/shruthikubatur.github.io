Reliable AI, from perception to autonomy.

Clare Boothe Luce Assistant Professor of Computer Science, The University of Texas at El Paso

My current focus is how AI agents fail. Most AI systems are evaluated one input at a time: a prompt goes in, an answer comes out, and we score the answer. AI agents do not work that way. They plan, call tools, and act over many steps, so a small error early in a task can compound into a failure that looks nothing like where it started.

My research builds models of these failure trajectories. I use tools from rare-event simulation and large deviation theory to model how failures unfold across the steps of a multi-step agentic workflow, and then use those models to predict, analyze, and trace failure pathways back to their origin. The goal is to make agent failures rare, predictable, and diagnosable rather than surprising.

Before joining UTEP in 2026, I spent eight years building computer vision and multimodal models in industry, including five at Apple, where I led work on vision-language models for spatial reasoning and on efficient on-device vision. I hold a PhD in Electrical Engineering from Purdue University, where I worked with Mary Comer on stochastic modeling and rare-event simulation. I am also the founder of Causyx, a startup working on trajectory-level risk analysis for AI agents.
